BookEasy HK Brand Guide

Quiet premium booking software for Hong Kong service businesses, with an editorial, bilingual, mobile-first visual language.

BookEasy HK combines calm hospitality cues with dependable SaaS structure. The customer-facing UI feels soft, polished, and approachable, while the merchant dashboard introduces darker contrast and operational clarity.

Typography

The brand personality comes from the tension between a refined serif headline system and a practical, highly readable sans-serif body system.

Display Font
Fraunces, Georgia, serif
Body Font
Plus Jakarta Sans, Noto Sans TC, system sans-serif fallbacks
Hero Heading
Text size: 5xl on mobile, 6xl on large screens. Weight: light. Tracking: tight. Line-height: approximately 1.1.
Section Headings
Usually 4xl in the display font, maintaining a soft editorial hierarchy.
Page Titles
Commonly 2xl bold in the product dashboard and settings screens.
Body Copy
Typically set between 14px and 15px, with supporting labels at 12px and utility metadata at 10px to 11px.

Visual Language

UI Structure

Customer-facing surfaces use soft ivory backgrounds, white cards, thin gray borders, rounded corners, and restrained elevation. The dashboard contrasts this with a dark sidebar and light work area, giving the system a more professional operator mode without breaking the wider brand.

Component Style

Cards typically use 12px to 16px radii. Buttons lean on rounded large rectangles with teal fills or outlined neutral treatments. Borders are preferred over heavy fills, and shadows stay subtle to preserve a light, polished feel.

Layout Direction

The interface relies on generous spacing, editorial-scale headlines, compact product cards, and structured bordered grids. The booking flow preserves calm white surfaces and teal progress states, while pricing and feature sections use simple modular blocks instead of decorative noise.

Motion

Animation is intentionally understated. Fade-up enters at roughly 0.5 seconds, fade-in at roughly 0.4 seconds. Motion supports clarity and polish rather than spectacle.
